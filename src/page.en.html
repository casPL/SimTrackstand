Controls

  • A D: hips left and right over the pegs. With the foot down they move while you hold the key and stay where you leave them. On the pegs a key moves them 8 cm towards its side, and when you let go they return to where they were before you stood up.
  • ← →: bars. They turn while you hold the key and stay where you leave them.
  • Space: start, then stand up on the pegs. P: pause.
  • The dot on the lean gauge, on the asphalt under the bike and in the support panel is the centre of gravity: green in the balance zone, yellow just beside it, red far outside it. The ring in the panel shows where it will be a quarter of a second later.
  • On a phone the buttons sit under the picture, and on top of it in landscape. The middle one starts, stands up and pauses.

What the bike stands on

A standing bike rests only on the two contact patches of its tyres. Between them lies a narrow support band, a few centimetres wide. As long as the centre of gravity of the bike and rider stays over that band, the tyres hold it up. Once it leaves the band, the bike falls over like an inverted pendulum.

In the game the balance zone is twice as wide as that band. On a real bike you feel it move with your body and correct it at once, while you react to a picture on a screen later. The wider zone makes up for that.

Tyres are not rigid: as the bike leans, the pressure moves across the patch until it reaches the edge. Beyond that only the round side of the tyre holds the bike up.

Starting with a foot down

At first the bike rests on your left foot and leans slightly to the left. The number in the top left corner is the load on the foot. Hips to the right reduce it. Bars turned left, towards the foot, add to it, and bars turned right reduce it.

Stand up when the load is close to zero. The bike is then in balance and does not fall to either side straight away.

Balancing on the pegs

Moving the hips works at once, because the bike rolls the other way. When the bike leans to the right, press the left peg and move your hips to the left. The bike leans further under you, and the pressure on the tyres moves to the right, under the centre of gravity.

Hips moved towards the lean right the bike only for a moment. The support of the tyres then moves away from under the centre of gravity and the bike keeps falling.

Bars

The wheels stand still and the front tyre cannot slide sideways. When you turn the bars, the frame turns about the rear tyre and the centre of gravity moves towards the turn, by about 3 cm at full lock. That is why a bike resting on your left leg sits on the leg harder with the bars turned left than with the bars turned right, and stands more securely on its side stand with the bars turned left.

The movement of the bars works the other way, and at once. When you turn the bars a little further towards the side the bike leans to, the bike straightens slightly, by about 0.4° for every 10° of steering. At full lock the bars have nowhere left to go, so this does not happen.

Bars turned further, though, move the centre of gravity towards the lean, so the straightening lasts only a moment and the hips have to do the rest. Turning the bars the other way works the other way round: it first deepens the lean a little, then brings the centre of gravity back over the tyres.

Support band and balance zone

bars to the leftto the right

Contact patches to scale, at the pressure chosen above the game. At 2 bar the front patch is about the size of a credit card and the rear one is wider. The lower the pressure, the longer the patch. The turned front tyre turns its patch, widens the support band a little and moves it sideways. The band between the patches is grey, and the balance zone of the game, twice as wide, is green.

Tyre pressure

Lower pressure gives a larger patch: the tyre flattens out more in length than in width. At the front, with the bars turned, that length stands across the bike, so the support band grows the most. A soft tyre keeps moving the pressure across its patch for longer before the bike rests on its round side.

On the road, too low a pressure is bad for the handling and for the tyre. Here it is only about balance on the spot.

Why it is so hard

A trackstand is an inverted pendulum. That is why corrections have to be small and early.

When you really ride, you feel the bike move with your body and react faster than to a picture on a screen. Game speed 50% and 35% slows time down, while the physics stays the same. Records are kept separately for every pressure and game speed.
